Save this one. Within five years — or less — remote supervision is coming to community pharmacy.

There's a tender doing the rounds: ~£360,000 to deliver clinical-skills training for pharmacy technicians in London, tied to Pharmacy First. On its own you could call it routine workforce commissioning. I don't read it that way.

When a system starts paying serious money to train technicians in clinical skills, it's telling you what it wants them to do next — and what it wants the more expensive professional above them to do instead.

Here's my read of where it lands:
→ One pharmacist looking after multiple shops — superintendent-style, but operational.
→ Day-to-day responsibility delegated to technicians trained up to Level 4.
→ The pharmacist supervising across sites, doing the clinical and the complex.

A lot of pharmacists watched my video on this and went straight to "we won't have jobs." That's not true — but whether it's true FOR YOU depends on one thing: are you developing, or standing still?

Keep doing nothing but dispensing, never moving toward prescribing, never upskilling — and yes, you'll struggle, because you've made yourself the exact thing the system is training technicians to do more cheaply. Upskill — and it takes time, so start now — and you'll have a bigger role than ever: the prescriber, the clinician, the one supervising the model.

We've seen this film before. Ask a GP. Policy changed, pharmacist and nurse prescribers took on work GPs used to hold, and a GP told me recently the job market is genuinely tough now — practices taking on pharmacist prescribers partly for financial reasons. The lesson cuts both ways: every profession is being reshaped, no one is exempt, and the winners are the ones who adapt.

To be clear — this is my prediction of the direction of travel, not a policy announcement. Supervision rules are still evolving, so check current GPhC guidance. But the ingredients are all on the table.
