"You can't build a chain of pharmacies on a small budget — one decent shop costs £500k-plus."

True. If your plan is to buy shops.

But that was never the plan. You don't need £200k to own a chain outright. You need it to control one — because what you're really acquiring isn't bricks and fittings. It's NHS contracts: stable, government-backed, bankable income streams.

Buy the contract, not the shop.

Here's why the reframe works:

→ The contract is the asset. Regular, protected, scarce income the government pays every month. That's why even a pharmacy losing £100k a year still sells for £200k-plus — you're not buying the loss, you're buying what a competent operator can do with the contract.

→ The cheap contracts are the unloved ones. Retiring, burnt-out or squeezed owners with no succession plan will sometimes sell for little over stock value just to get out cleanly. You solve their problem; they hand you an asset.

→ Structure so barely any of your cash leaves the room. Seller finance, deferred consideration, earn-outs, staged payments — pay out of the business's future profit, not your pocket. Your £200k becomes deposits and working capital across several deals, not the full price of one.

→ Leverage + partners multiply your reach. Banks lend against the NHS contract. Investors bring capital while you bring the operating expertise and the regulatory standing. Sweat equity across several sites is a real route to a group.

Now the honest part, because I won't sell you a fantasy:

This is hustle, not passive wealth. The NHS cash-flow lag multiplies across every site and can strangle a group that looks profitable on paper. TUPE follows every deal. Personal guarantees put your own assets on the line. And the old easy routes in are closed — this is about acquiring EXISTING contracts smartly, in a market where entry is still tightly controlled.

Under £200k doesn't buy a chain. It's the equity and the hustle that lets you assemble and control one — if you understand that the contract is the asset.
